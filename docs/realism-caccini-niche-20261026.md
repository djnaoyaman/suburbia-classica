# Ave Maria — a small Marian niche

The work commonly labelled “Caccini’s Ave Maria” is credited to the twentieth-century composer Vladimir Vavilov, not to the Renaissance/early-Baroque composer Giulio Caccini. Its original repeated words address Mary. The small blue-veiled Marian sculpture and votive therefore make that invocation visible, rather than attaching a generic candle or shell to the music. [GIA composer attribution](https://giamusic.com/resource/ave-maria-print-g6169), [Nicholas Palmer’s edition note on the original text](https://www.jwpepper.com/ave-maria-11377712/p).

This generated photographic scene is a contemporary editorial interpretation, not a named statue, actual devotional site or historical Baroque room. The close warm stone-and-blue composition differs from a broad church interior. The sculpture remains completely still: only the small flame tip and light within the votive vary gently. The wick, glass, shelf, face and camera do not move. There is no simulated singing or gesture.

The selected recording is unchanged; the artist’s official album listing identifies Andrea Bocelli with Myung-Whun Chung and the Santa Cecilia orchestra/chorus. That listing retains the traditional Caccini/Mercurio label, which does not establish the work’s true authorship. [Official album listing](https://shopandreabocelli.com/products/sacred-arias-cd).
