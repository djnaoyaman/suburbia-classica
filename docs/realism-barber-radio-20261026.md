# Barber — Adagio for Strings, Op. 11: a radio listening scene

The orchestral Adagio reached a national audience through Toscanini and the NBC Symphony Orchestra in 1938. A period-inspired wooden radio makes that broadcasting history the visual link, replacing an unrelated animal. The work began as the slow movement of Barber’s string quartet, not as a funeral programme. [Library of Congress programme, PDF page 12](https://www.loc.gov/static/events/concerts-from-the-library-of-congress/documents/programs/2425-Pacifica-Strad-Dec18-Dec19-program.pdf).

This generated photographic interpretation is an imagined listening space, not Barber’s room, a documented radio model, or a reconstruction of the broadcast. The radio’s cabinet, grille and controls draw on the general construction of 1930s wooden receivers. [SFO Museum: On the Radio](https://www.sfomuseum.org/exhibitions/radio/gallery).

Only sparse vapor above the cup rises and disperses. Radio, dial, cup, furniture and camera stay still; there is no simulated loudspeaker motion or synchronization to individual notes. Portrait framing is a closer view of the radio and cup. The selected recording is unchanged and is not presented as the 1938 broadcast. Reduced motion holds a still frame; if the photographic scene is unavailable, the existing fallback remains.
