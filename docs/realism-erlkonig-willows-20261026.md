# Erlkönig — night wind, grey willows and mist

Goethe’s text, set by Schubert, gives the father’s natural explanations for the child’s frightening perceptions: mist, wind in withered leaves, and old grey willows. These concrete images replace an unrelated bat. [Oxford Song: Schubert’s Erlkönig, text and translation](https://oxfordsong.org/song/erlk%C3%B6nig-2).

This generated photographic night path is an editorial reading of that uncertain boundary between what is seen and what is feared. It is not a documented location visited by Goethe or Schubert, and does not settle the story’s supernatural ambiguity. No rider, child, death, or supernatural figure is depicted.

Only a small group of hanging twigs and leaves stirs, while thin low mist drifts over the middle-distance path. The trunks, ground geometry and camera remain fixed; mist may gently veil what lies behind it without deforming the image. There is no simulated gallop, camera rush or note-synchronized gesture. Portrait framing retains the path and middle-distance willow. The selected recording and timing remain unchanged; reduced motion holds a still frame, with the existing fallback available if needed.
