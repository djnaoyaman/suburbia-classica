# Ave verum corpus, K. 618 — a quiet Eucharistic still life

Mozart’s motet sets a hymn to Christ’s true body, born of Mary, sacrificed on the cross and invoked at the trial of death. Its Eucharistic meaning—not merely white coloration—motivates the chalice and communion bread on linen. [Boston Baroque programme note and text](https://baroque.boston/mozart-618), [Mozarteum’s Köchel catalogue](https://kv.mozarteum.at/de/work/ave-verum-corpus-in-d-7132).

This generated photographic still life is an editorial interpretation, not a depiction of an actual consecration, documented sacred vessels, Mozart’s Baden church or a historical ceremony. Its close, restrained ivory-and-silver composition keeps the focus on the text rather than a grand church interior.

Only a lower loose fold of linen moves gently. The chalice, paten, bread, supporting surface and camera remain fixed; there is no simulated ritual or note-synchronized gesture. Portrait and wide crops retain both vessels and the bread. The selected recording is unchanged. Reduced motion holds a still frame and the existing fallback remains available.
