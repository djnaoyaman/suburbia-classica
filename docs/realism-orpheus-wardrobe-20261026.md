# Orpheus in the Underworld overture — backstage wardrobe

The familiar concert overture is Carl Binder’s adaptation of Offenbach’s operetta, distinct from the short introduction to the 1858 opera and Offenbach’s later overture. It contains more than the famous fast finale. [Offenbach Edition Keck critical report, PDF pages 7 and 27](https://www.boosey.com/downloads/Orphee58_CriticalReport_E.pdf).

The final-act Galop infernal became especially familiar as can-can music. That reception—not a claim that the opera’s original dance was identical to the later can-can—motivates this blue-and-white flounced costume waiting backstage. [English National Opera’s introduction](https://www.eno.org/discover-opera/articles/an-introduction-to-orpheus-in-the-underworld/).

This is a generated photographic interpretation of a contemporary wardrobe preparation space, not an authentic 1858 costume, a named production, or a reconstruction of Binder’s premiere. There is no dancer or simulated choreography. Only a few interior cotton tiers move gently in imagined ventilation; the bodice, garment’s outer silhouette, support, room and camera stay fixed. The centered garment and its support remain visible in portrait and wide framing. The selected music is unchanged. Reduced motion holds a still frame, and the existing fallback remains if the scene cannot load.
